One-way video interview checklist: the invitation, setup and how a recorded answer differs

In a one-way (on-demand) video interview, you record your answers to questions shown on a screen, at a time you choose (usually before a deadline), and the employer reviews them later. HireVue is one of the platforms employers use for them. I'm not affiliated with HireVue or any interview platform.

What HireVue's own pages say (read October 8, 2026)

  • Its candidate tips page: “You'll even have the opportunity to do a practice question.” “Most on-demand interviews ask you to give your answer in just a few minutes.” “It's helpful to think about how you will illustrate your skills clearly in that timeframe, and to practice them out loud.”
  • Its FAQ recommends an internet download and upload speed of “at least 350 Kbps per second” (0.35 Mbps) for its on-demand interviews, lists “computer, tablet, phone” as devices to choose from, and says that if you lose your connection in the middle, you can “pick up where you left off” with the same link.

What those two pages don't say: how long you get to think before each answer, the exact time limit for each answer, or whether you can re-record. Don't assume any of them: read your invitation and every screen before you press record.

Sources: hirevue.com/candidates/interview-tips and hirevue.com/candidates/faq. Pages change, so check them yourself.

Before you start: read the invitation

Write down the deadline, the number of questions, the thinking and answer time, whether there's a practice question and whether you can re-record, wherever the invitation or the screens say. If something isn't stated, prepare for the strictest case: one take and a short answer time.

The day before: set up

  • Device: pick the one you're most comfortable with. Charge it, and keep it plugged in if you can.
  • Connection: test your internet speed, and close other apps and downloads.
  • Camera and light: camera at eye level, light in front of you (not behind), a plain background, a quiet room, notifications off.
  • Notes: a few keywords next to the camera, not a script. Reading tends to show on video.
  • The practice question: if there is one, use it to check your sound, light and framing, not only your answer.

How a one-way answer differs from a live one

  • Nobody can ask a follow-up. Each answer has to stand on its own: the situation in a sentence, what you did, the result and why it matters for this job.
  • Nobody nods or interrupts. Silence isn't a signal. Start with your main point and stop when you've made it; you don't have to use all the time.
  • There's usually a time limit. Practice to a limit a little shorter than the one you expect, so a slow start doesn't cut off your result.
  • Look at the lens, not at your own picture.

If something goes wrong

  • Your connection drops: on HireVue, its FAQ says you can pick up where you left off with the same link. On another platform, check its help page or your invitation.
  • Anything else (a crash, a question you can't see or hear, a deadline you can't meet): contact the recruiter, or the contact named in your invitation, as soon as you can (ideally before the deadline), and say plainly what happened.
  • If you need an accommodation, such as more time, ask the employer before you start.
