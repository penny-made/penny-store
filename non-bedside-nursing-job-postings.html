Non-bedside nursing jobs: what 23 real job postings had in common (checked Oct 7, 2026)

On Oct 7, 2026, I read 23 job postings for nurses outside hospital bedside roles, all still posted on public job boards that day (one listed a contract period that had already passed). Most were on employers' own job boards, plus one recruiter's ad. The employers were mostly health-tech companies, health plans and outpatient care groups, plus a drug-company support firm, a documentation consulting firm, a nursing school and a publisher. Big hospital systems weren't in the sample. That's a small sample from one day, so use this page to learn what to look for, then check postings where you live.

What the postings had in common

The first three points are about the 11 postings for utilization review, case management, triage and medical review.

  • A license, and which one. All 11 asked for an RN license (two also accepted a social-work credential instead). Several wanted a license from one specific state (California in four, Michigan in one). One required a multistate (compact) license, and others accepted or preferred one. Read the license line first.
  • Certifications were rarely the gate. None of the 11 required a certification. Several listed one as preferred (for example CCM).
  • Experience in the specialty was the gate. 9 of the 11 required utilization review or management, case management, care management or care coordination experience already, from one year up to “3-5+ years”.
  • Two outpatient postings said experience wasn't required. An oncology clinic said two or more years as an RN was “preferred but not required”, and a recruiter's ad for a clinic said previous experience was “ideal but not required”. An infusion center asked for hands-on skills instead (strong IV and infusion skills, including experience with specialty biologics) and named no number of years.

How to check a role

  1. Search a job site for the title plus your city, or plus “remote”.
  2. Open 3-5 recent postings. Read the license line first: which state, and is a multistate license accepted?
  3. Write down what more than one posting asks for: years and type of experience, certifications (required or preferred), schedule, remote or on site.
  4. Mark each requirement: I have it, my bedside work shows it, or I'd need to build it.

If most marks are in the third group, look at outpatient and clinic nursing, or at roles that build the experience the others ask for.

What this page isn't

This isn't medical, legal or licensing advice. For anything about your license, read your own state board of nursing's rules.
