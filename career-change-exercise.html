Career change exercise: sort what you want to leave (job, employer, field or life)

Before choosing something new, get clear on what you want to leave. The right fix depends on where the problem sits.

Step 1: the leave list (15 minutes)

Write down everything you'd change about your work right now. Don't filter; aim for 10-20 lines. Include small things (“the commute”, “Monday meetings”) and big ones (“I don't believe in what we make”).

Step 2: tag each line (10 minutes)

Put one tag next to each line:

  • J (the job): the tasks themselves. They'd follow you to the same role at another employer. Example: “I spend most of the day on the phone.”
  • E (the employer): this manager, team, culture, pay policy, schedule rules or location. Another employer could be different. Example: “My manager changes priorities every week.”
  • F (the field): the work or the industry as a whole, wherever you'd do it. Example: “I don't want to work in sales at all.”
  • L (life): things outside work that the job collides with, such as health, caring for someone or a move. Example: “Night shifts don't work with my kids' school hours.”

If a line could take two tags, pick the one that feels most true and move on.

Step 3: count and read (10 minutes)

Count the tags, then answer each question in a sentence or two:

  1. Which tag has the most lines?
  2. Which three lines bother you most, and what are their tags?
  3. If you kept your field but changed employer, which lines would go away?
  4. If you kept your employer but changed role, which lines would go away?

How to read your answers

These are rules of thumb, not facts about careers:

  • Mostly E: a new employer in the same field may fix most of it. That's often the faster, lower-risk move, because you don't have to retrain.
  • Mostly J: look at roles next to yours in the same field, with a different job title that uses what you already know.
  • Mostly F: a career change is likely the real answer.
  • Mostly L: you may need a different schedule, location or workload more than a different career. Write down your must-haves first, and let them filter every option.
  • A mix: keep your top three lines and check every option you consider against them.

Example (made up)

Dana, a retail store manager, wrote 14 lines: 3 tagged E, 2 J, 6 F and 3 L. Dana's top three were “on my feet all day” (J), “weekends and holidays” (F, because Dana expects that in any retail job) and “missing evenings with my kids” (L). A different store wouldn't fix any of them, so Dana kept going toward a career change, with “weekday daytime hours” as a must-have.

Keep your top three lines, with their tags, for whatever you look at next.
