Resume prompts for ChatGPT, Claude or Gemini: match the posting, catch invented claims (free)

Chatbots are fast at resumes. They're also quick to add a skill you don't have, turn “helped with” into “led”, or make up a number. This page has five prompts: two that read a job posting and match it against your experience before you change a word, and three checks to run on the finished resume.

Every prompt here is plain text, so you can paste it into ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot or another chatbot you use. Remove your contact details (address, phone number, email) from anything you paste into a chatbot. The prompts don't need them. Add them back in your final document.

Step 1: read the job posting with the chatbot

Before you change a word of your resume, find out what this employer actually asks for. Paste the full posting, including the “about us” part.

Prompt: break the posting down

Here is a job posting: [paste the full posting]. Break it down into: (1) the must-have requirements, in the posting's own words; (2) the nice-to-haves; (3) the 8-12 skills, tools and terms that appear most often or most prominently; (4) in two sentences, the main problem this person is being hired to solve, based only on the posting. Don't guess at things the posting doesn't say; if something is unclear, say it's unclear.

Prompt: match it against your facts

Here is my master career document: [paste]. Using the breakdown above, make three lists: (A) requirements my document clearly supports, with the item that supports each; (B) requirements my document partly supports, with what's missing; (C) requirements my document doesn't support at all. Be strict: if an item only loosely relates, put it in B or C, not A.

A master career document is one complete, factual list of your experience, written from rough notes. No master career document yet? Paste your current resume in its place. It may leave out work you've done, so check list C against your own memory.

What to do with the result

  • List A is your resume's backbone for this job. Those items go near the top.
  • List B is where better wording helps: you may have done the work but described it in other words.
  • List C is honest information. If it's mostly must-haves, this job may be a stretch. That's your call to make; the chatbot shouldn't paper over it.

Step 2: tailor the resume

Rewrite your summary, bullets and skills line for this posting, on your own or with a chatbot, using list A first. The checks below work either way.

Step 3: three checks before you send

Use a new conversation for the first one, so the chatbot judges the two documents rather than the drafts it helped write. (Some apps remember past chats; if yours does, you can usually turn that off in its settings.)

1. The invented-claims check

Below are two documents: (1) my master career document and (2) my tailored resume. Compare them line by line. List every statement in the resume that isn't directly supported by the master document: any skill, tool, title, number, result or level of responsibility that is new, bigger or vaguer than the source. For each, quote the resume line and the closest master-document item. Don't suggest improvements yet; only find mismatches.

(1) [paste master career document]
(2) [paste tailored resume]

No master career document yet? Paste your current resume as (1) and the chatbot's rewrite as (2).

Fix or delete every mismatch the check finds. If something is true but missing from your master document, add it there first, so your source stays complete.

2. The 30-second skim test

Act as a busy hiring manager for this role: [paste posting title and must-haves]. You have 30 seconds to skim this resume: [paste]. Tell me: (1) what role you think I'm aiming for, (2) the two strongest things you noticed, (3) anything that confused you or made you stop, (4) one must-have you couldn't find. Be blunt and brief.

If the answer to (1) isn't the job you're applying for, your summary needs work. If (4) names something you do have, move it higher.

3. The format check

Here's my resume as plain text: [paste]. Check it for: (1) typos and inconsistent tense (past roles in past tense), (2) inconsistent date formats, (3) the same verb used more than twice, (4) bullets over 25 words, (5) anything that only makes sense with formatting (icons, columns, text in images). List problems only; don't rewrite.

If you're unsure about layout, a simple one-column resume with standard headings (“Experience”, “Education”, “Skills”) is a cautious choice. No layout guarantees anything, so be wary of anyone promising that one does.

What this page isn't

No prompt or layout can promise interviews, offers or getting past any particular screening system.
