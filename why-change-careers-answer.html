Why are you changing careers? A 30-second answer formula, template and example (free)

Changing careers means explaining, again and again, why someone from a different field is a good bet. This page has a short way to answer “why the change?”: a 4-part formula, a template to fill with true details, a made-up example, and lines for your 30s, 40s or 50s.

Expect “why the change?” in interviews and in chats with people in your new field. A good answer is short (about 30 seconds), looks forward rather than complaining about the old job, and ends with what you bring.

The 4-part formula

  1. Where you've been: one line on your background.
  2. What pulled you: the moment or interest that pointed you to the new field (true and specific).
  3. What you've done about it: a course, a project, volunteering, a piece of work, conversations with people in the field.
  4. What you bring: one or two strengths from your past work that carry over.

Template

I spent [N years] as a [old role], where I [one strength-related task]. Over the last [period], I got more and more interested in [new field] because [true reason]. I've [action: completed X / built Y / volunteered at Z], and I found I really enjoy [specific part]. I'm now looking for a [target role] where I can use my [carry-over strength] while I grow in [new skill].

Example (made up): teacher → instructional designer

I taught middle-school science for eight years, where I designed lessons for students with very different needs. In the last year I got interested in how adults learn at work, after building our school's training for new teachers. I've since finished two online courses in instructional design and built three sample modules. I'm looking for an instructional designer role where I can use my lesson-planning experience while I build my skills in e-learning tools.

If age comes up, or you're worried it will

Don't apologize for your years; use them.

  • 30s: “I've built a solid base in [old field], and I'm choosing [new field] deliberately, with a plan, not on impulse.”
  • 40s: “I bring [N] years of working with [people / budgets / deadlines], and I'm making this move with my eyes open: I've already [action].”
  • 50s: “I've got [N] years of experience in [strength], and I want to spend the next part of my career on [new field]. I'm someone who stays: I was at [last employer] for [N] years.”

Only use a line if it's true for you.

Practice

Say it out loud three times, then record yourself once. Cut any sentence that explains why you left your old job. Your story is about where you're going.

What this page isn't

No answer can promise interviews or offers. If your target field requires a license or certificate you don't have yet, get that first.
