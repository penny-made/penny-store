Cover letter prompts for ChatGPT, Claude or Gemini that stick to your own facts (free)

Ask a chatbot for a cover letter and you'll get one in seconds. It may also sound like everyone else's, or mention a skill you don't have. This page has four prompts. The first turns your rough notes into a facts sheet, so the chatbot works from true material. The other three check a finished letter before you send it.

Every prompt here is plain text, so you can paste it into ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot or another chatbot you use. Remove your contact details (address, phone number, email) from anything you paste into a chatbot. The prompts don't need them. Add them to the final letter yourself.

Step 1: your facts sheet (once)

A cover letter uses only a few of your facts, so they should be your best ones. Your facts sheet holds four things the prompts draw from. Write it once and add to it after each application.

  • Proof (8-15 lines). Things you've done that you'd be glad to talk about, one per line, in the form “what I did + how much or how often + what changed”. A made-up example: “answered about 60 customer emails a day; cut our reply backlog from a week to two days”. Numbers help, but only true ones.
  • True reasons (3-6 lines). Why you want this kind of work, in plain words. These become the “why” sentences in your letter, so the chatbot doesn't have to invent enthusiasm.
  • Practical facts (only what you'd share with an employer). The job title you hold now (or held last), how many years you've done this kind of work, a certificate you've actually earned, whether you're relocating, your earliest start date.
  • A sample of how you write (about 100-200 words). Something you wrote yourself in a normal, professional tone: a work email, a message to a colleague, a short post. Remove names and private details first.

Then let the chatbot tidy it:

I'm building a facts sheet for writing cover letters. Below are my rough notes in four parts: proof, reasons, practical facts and a writing sample. Tidy parts 1-3 into short one-line items. For each proof item, keep my numbers exactly; if an item would be stronger with a number or result I didn't give, add [number?] or [result?] instead of guessing. Don't add skills, tools, titles or achievements that aren't in my notes. Leave part 4 (my writing sample) exactly as written. At the end, ask me up to 3 questions that would most improve my proof items.

[paste your notes]

Answer the questions, fill or delete each bracket, and save the result as one document.

Step 2: the letter

Write the letter from your facts sheet and the job posting, on your own or with a chatbot. The checks below work either way.

Step 3: three checks before you send

Use a new conversation for check 1, so the check works only from your facts sheet and your letter, not from the conversation that wrote the draft. (Some apps remember past chats; if yours does, look in its settings for a way to turn that off.)

1. The invented-claims check

Below are two documents: (1) my facts sheet and (2) my cover letter. Compare them sentence by sentence. List every statement in the letter that isn't directly supported by the facts sheet: any skill, tool, title, number, result, level of responsibility or reason for wanting the job that is new, bigger or vaguer than the source. For each, quote the letter's sentence and the closest facts-sheet item, or say “nothing close”. Don't suggest improvements; only find mismatches.

(1) [paste facts sheet]
(2) [paste cover letter]

Fix or delete every mismatch. If something is true but missing from your facts sheet, add it there first, so your source stays complete.

2. The posting-match check

Here is the job posting: [paste]. Here is my cover letter: [paste]. Answer briefly: (1) Which of the posting's three most important requirements does the letter address, and where? (2) Which of them, if any, does it miss? (3) Did the posting ask applicants to include anything (a link, an answer, a salary expectation), and does the letter or my application need to include it? (4) Is any sentence about the company something the posting or my notes don't actually say?

If (2) names something you truly have, add it in one sentence with proof. If you don't have it, leave it out; the letter shouldn't pretend.

3. The names-and-details check

List every company name, person's name, job title, date and bracket like [check] that appears in this letter: [paste]. Don't change anything.

Then compare the list with the posting yourself: the company name spelled the way they write it, the job title exactly as posted, the right person's name (or “Hiring Team” if the posting names no one), and no name left over from a letter you wrote for another job.

What this page isn't

No prompt can promise interviews or offers, or get a letter past any particular screening system.
